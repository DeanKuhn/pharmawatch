Before drawing conclusions

  • FAERS reports are voluntary and unverified. A report does not show the drug caused the reaction.
  • There is no denominator: FAERS doesn't know how many people took the drug, so these are not rates or risks.
  • IC compares this pair with all other drugs in FAERS. A high IC means "reported unusually often", not "dangerous".
  • News, recalls and lawsuits drive bursts of reports. Check the lawyer share and the peak quarter.
  • The reaction may come from the condition being treated rather than the drug.
  • Duplicate reports are reduced but not eliminated. Drug names are matched from free text.
